ESURV - GEAR, EXPLAINED
============================

This file explains, in plain words, how gear works and how it is organised. The technical
version is gear.md in the folder above this one.

Class gear is now worked out: what each piece gives and how it grows from zone to zone
(section 3).
Still open: what happens to a piece for a slot you already fill ("not known yet").


1. THE FIVE KINDS OF GEAR
-------------------------

  STARTING OUTFIT
      What a brand new hero wears: plain clothes and a basic weapon, like in WoW at level 1.

  CLASS GEAR (armor and a weapon)
      Bosses drop pieces of your class's set. You keep them for good, across runs.
      This is the hero's long-term progression.

  RINGS
      Found during a run, in Battleground Caches and rares' caches. There are 9 rings.
      Each ring gives one bonus, for example "+17% damage".
      Lost when the run ends; every run starts without rings. (Seen in play.)

  TRINKETS
      Found the same way as rings. There are 13 trinkets, all real WoW trinkets (Hand of
      Justice, Carrot on a Stick, Onyxia Blood Talisman, Soulstone, ...). Also lost when the
      run ends. (Seen in play; other details
      not known yet.)

  AMULETS (necklaces)
      17 of them, plus LEGENDARY amulets that remake one of your spells (see below).
      You can wear one at a time; taking a new one replaces the old one.
      If the game offers you the same amulet again while you wear it, it gets stronger
      ("empowered"), growing toward three times its strength.
      Also lost when the run ends. (Seen in play.)

  IN SHORT: class gear is what your hero keeps and grows over many runs. Rings, trinkets and
  amulets are only for the current run, like the spells you pick when levelling up.


1b. HOW YOU GET GEAR DURING A RUN (seen in play)
------------------------------------------------

  BOSSES
      When a boss dies, it can drop a piece of your class gear. The chance goes by the zone's
      difficulty D, in steps (little grinding for gear):

          Elwynn 100%   Duskwood and Warsong 80%   Arathi and Dire Maul 70%
          Gurubashi 60%   Stratholme, Blackrock and Ahn'Qiraj 40%

      The steps are in content\balance.ron, zone_scaling, gear_drop_chance.

      Harder Run Options multiply this, up to x2. Enemy SPEED counts the most, then damage,
      then health; density barely counts.

      Each zone drops its own whole set, stronger in harder zones (each
      zone its own names and looks). Each of the 4 normal bosses drops two
      armor pieces; the final boss drops the weapon.

  CACHES (bought ones and rares' free ones)
      Opening one shows 3 choices, just like a level-up, drawn from rings, trinkets and
      necklaces. You pick ONE. An offer can be:
          - a NEW item for a free slot,
          - an UPGRADE to an item you already wear (it goes up a rank), or
          - a NECKLACE that takes the place of the one you wear.

  RARITY - THE SAME IDEA AS THE SPELL CARDS
      Every offer rolls a rarity (white, green, blue, purple, orange). The rarity decides:
          - for an upgrade: HOW MUCH better the next rank is (higher rarity = bigger jump)
          - for a new item: what rarity the item has
      The top rarity, LEGENDARY (orange), is the biggest jump for a ring or trinket; for a
      necklace it can be a legendary necklace. Legendary necklaces only appear when a
      necklace offer rolls legendary.

  FOUND IN THE CODE:

    How likely each rarity is (the same rule for spell cards and cache items):

                       spell cards,       necklaces
                       rings, trinkets    from a cache
        white             60%                43%
        green             25%                27%
        blue              10%                16%
        purple             4%                10%
        orange             1%                 4%

      (at the very start with no luck; luck and level raise the rare colours - level counts
      up to level 30. Necklaces always get an extra bonus, so they roll rarer than rings and
      trinkets, and a rare cache raises the odds of everything it offers a little more.)

    What a cache can show (3 different ones, picked at random, some more likely than others):
        - an upgrade to a ring/trinket you wear        (most likely)
        - an amulet                                     (likely)
        - "empower" your necklace, if you wear one
        - a new ring or trinket                         (least likely)

    You wear at most 2 rings and 2 trinkets. Once both ring slots are full, no new ring is
    offered any more (the two you wear can still be upgraded); the same for trinkets. A piece
    that raises buff power only comes once you have a shield, buff or aura spell, and one that
    adds damage-over-time stacks only once you have a damage-over-time spell.

    Upgrades stop being offered once an item is close to its maximum. Some bonuses have no
    maximum (damage, cooldowns, critical strike, area); others can grow to 3 or 5 times their
    base value, and a new one never starts above that.

    If there are fewer than 3 things to offer, a gold card fills the empty spot.

    HOW MUCH BETTER (the rarity decides it, as you noticed in play):

        colour          ring/trinket strength      spell card
        white   x1.0                               no bonus
        green   x1.3                               +15% power  (or -7% cooldown)
        blue    x1.7                               +25% power  (or -10% cooldown)
        purple  x2.5                               +35% power  (or -13% cooldown)
        orange  x3.2                               +50% power  (or -17% cooldown)

      - A NEW ring or trinket starts at 1.5 x that strength x its normal value.
      - An UPGRADE adds that strength x its normal value, but each upgrade of the same item
        adds a bit less than the one before (8% less per upgrade already taken).
      - Bonuses that have a maximum grow more slowly as they get close to it.
      - Spells that do damage get more power; spells that don't (buffs, shields) get a
        shorter cooldown instead.

  WHERE CACHES, GOLD, RUNES AND MAGNETS COME FROM

    Price:  opening a cache costs gold: 20 for the first, then 30, 42, 57, 76, ... (each
            price is the last one x 1.25 + 5).
    Caches: 2 lie on the field when a run starts, and a new one appears every 70 seconds
            while there are fewer than 3. Every ELITE you kill drops one. Killing a RARE
            drops a RARE'S CACHE, free to open (no gold; the price of the others stays).
    Gold:   normal enemies sometimes drop 2-4 gold; elites always drop 15; rares and
            bosses drop more (and more each time).
    Runes:  every 45 seconds a rune may appear (if at most one is lying around):
              Speed Rune        +60% movement speed for 10 seconds
              Berserker Rune    +50% damage for 15 seconds
              Restoration Rune  heals half your health
    Magnets: 2 or 3 per run, spread over the run. One shows up with a beam of light; taking
            it pulls everything on the field to you. Killing a boss (or, half the time, a
            rare) can make the next magnet drop early.

    Picking up:
            Experience orbs fly to you when you come close (your pickup range). Orbs you
            leave behind come to you by themselves after 6 seconds, wherever they are.
            Gold is picked up as soon as you come close (pickup range).
            Caches, runes and magnets: walk right onto them. A cache only opens if you
            have enough gold; otherwise it stays where it is.

  AMULETS IN DETAIL

    Each amulet has a colour (rarity) and gives one to three bonuses, written like talents
    (for example "All damage +15%", or "A blow that would kill you leaves you at 50% health
    instead, once a minute"). They are listed in content\amulets.ron.

    When a cache offers an amulet, it rolls a colour and then picks any amulet of that exact
    colour. You are never offered the amulet you already wear as a "new" one.

    LEGENDARY AMULETS remake one spell you know. There is one recipe per KIND of spell (the
    "delivery" kind in classes.txt), for example:
        projectile spells (Fireball...)  -> Eye of the Splitting Sky: +1 target, +20% damage,
                                            and each hit leaps to 2 more enemies for 30%
        melee strikes (Cleave...)        -> Medallion of the Bladestorm: +4 targets, +50%
                                            damage, and every hit makes the target bleed
        cones (Cone of Cold...)          -> Breath of the Black Dragon: wider, +30% damage,
                                            and every hit burns
    The amulet is called "<recipe name>: <spell name>", e.g. "Eye of the Splitting Sky:
    Fireball". The 11 recipes are in content\legendary_amulets.ron; gear.md lists them all.

    EMPOWER: when you are offered the amulet you already wear, taking it raises its rank and
    all its numbers grow:
        rank 1: x1.0    rank 2: x1.6    rank 3: x2.0    rank 4: x2.3   ... never past x3
    (that is for the fastest-growing amulets; some grow more slowly, legendary ones grow
    x1.2, x1.38, ...). A cooldown cut never goes past 50%.

  STILL TO FIND: what one totem amulet effect (TotemHeal) does.

Two screens show gear: the Armory (what your hero wears) and the Codex (every piece any run has
ever offered you).


2. THE STARTING OUTFIT
----------------------

Each class starts with real WoW starter items. Each item is described by a few numbers:

    name             Worn Shortsword
    item number      25     - the item's ID in WoW 1.12
    look             1542   - which 3D model it shows (WoW "display ID")
    slot             21     - where it is worn. Some slot numbers:
                                21 main hand      17 two-handed weapon    13 one-handed weapon
                                14 shield         15 ranged weapon         5 chest
                                 7 legs            8 feet                   4 shirt
    item type        2      - 2 = weapon, 4 = armor
    weapon kind      7      - 7 = one-handed sword, 10 = staff, 4 = one-handed mace,
                              0 = one-handed axe
    sheath           3      - how the weapon hangs on the back or belt when put away

Examples of starter items used: Recruit's Shirt, Footpad's Shoes, Apprentice's Robe,
Worn Shortsword, Bent Staff, Worn Mace.


3. CLASS GEAR SETS
------------------

EVERY ZONE HAS ITS OWN LOOK. Each class's set has a look per zone:
its 8 armor pieces with their own names and models, and the zone's ARMAMENTS: a weapon look for
each of the class's three weapon loadouts. Stats do not change with the look: they come from
the zone's difficulty (below).

    Zone (D)                Armor look                          Armaments
    Elwynn (1)              the Recruit's starting look         level 10-15 green weapons
    Duskwood (2)            Deadmines, Shadowfang Keep,         the same dungeons
                            Blackfathom Deeps, Gnomeregan
    Warsong Gulch (3)       Warsong reputation and level 25-35  Warsong reputation and others
    Scarlet Monastery (4)   "Adventurer's" pieces, level 30-45  Scarlet Monastery
    Sunken Temple (5)       Sunken Temple, Zul'Farrak,          the same dungeons
                            Maraudon, Uldaman
    Dire Maul (6)           Dungeon Set 1 (Valor, Lightforge..)  Dire Maul and Blackrock pre-raid
    Gurubashi Arena (7)     Zul'Gurub sets                      Zul'Gurub
    Stratholme (8)          TWO ROWS: Dungeon Set 2 (Heroism,   Stratholme drops
                            Soulforge..) and the Scholomance    Scholomance drops
                            sets
    Blackrock (9)           TWO ROWS: Tier 1 (Molten Core)      Molten Core
                            and Tier 2 (Blackwing Lair)         Blackwing Lair
    Ahn'Qiraj (10)          Tier 2.5                            Temple of Ahn'Qiraj
    (Naxxramas, no zone yet) Tier 3                             Naxxramas

The Naxxramas looks wait in the content for its zone.

BLACKROCK'S TWO ROWS: Blackrock's pieces have the same stats in
both rows. A Blackrock piece unlocks that piece's look in BOTH rows: it shows the Molten Core
row of its own, and you pick the Blackwing Lair row as a look (Show This Look) like any other.

STRATHOLME'S TWO ROWS: the same as Blackrock. Row 0 is Dungeon Set 2 with mostly Stratholme weapons; row 1, the
"Scholomance sets", is each armor type's Scholomance set (plate Deathbone Guardian, mail
Bloodmail Regalia, leather Cadaverous Garb, cloth Necropile Raiment), its missing slots
filled with Stratholme drops of the same colours, with mostly Scholomance weapons.
gear.md section 3 lists every piece and weapon.

Each zone has 8 ARMOR PIECES (head, shoulders, chest, waist, legs, feet, wrists, hands) and
the class WEAPONS (one weapon, or two for rogue = two daggers and shaman = mace + shield). A
weapon piece is called "<zone> Armaments": ONE of them unlocks the zone's weapon looks for all
three loadouts. What you hold is your chosen loadout's weapons, in the armaments of the weapon
piece you wear (its own zone's, or the look you picked for its slot; Elwynn's with none).

Quality colour: from the zone's difficulty: Elwynn common, Duskwood and Warsong
uncommon, Scarlet Monastery, Sunken Temple and Dire Maul rare, Gurubashi and Stratholme epic,
Blackrock and Ahn'Qiraj legendary.

WHAT A PIECE GIVES YOU

  EACH ZONE DROPS ITS OWN GEAR. A piece is a slot from a zone, with the zone's own name and
  look; its tooltip says where it came from: "From Duskwood".

  Every slot gives the SAME stats for every class. These are the Elwynn values:

      head        +1% crit chance, +4% crit damage
      shoulders   +3% area, +5 health
      chest       +15 health, +1% less damage taken
      waist       +10% pickup range, +5% gold
      legs        +10 health, +2% chance to cast twice
      feet        +2% move speed, +10 health
      wrists      +2% faster cooldowns, +2% heal and shield power
      hands       +10% luck
      weapon      +8% damage
      off-hand    +4% damage, +6 health   (rogue's second dagger, shaman's shield)

  HEAL AND SHIELD POWER is a stat of ours: it makes the heals of your spells (instant heals,
  heals over time, Bloodthirst's healing charges, spells that heal from their damage) and the
  shields you cast stronger. It does not touch health per second, lifesteal or health per kill.

  A harder zone makes every stat bigger:

      real value = Elwynn value  x  gear factor, rounded UP to a whole number
      gear factor = D ^ 0.55 up to D 9, then x 1.3 more for each step of D past 9

  D is the zone's difficulty (its file in content\zones). Percentages round UP to whole percents, health UP to
  whole points, so no gear stat ever shows a decimal. A zone added later gets its own gear
  level from its D, with nothing else to change. The numbers are in content\balance.ron,
  zone_scaling: each zone's set is meant to add about 10% damage over the one before, up to the
  set worn in Ahn'Qiraj, and about 25% a set after that.

      zone        D      x       a 10 that becomes   a 2% that becomes
      Elwynn      1      1.00    10                  2%
      Duskwood    2      1.46    15                  3%
      Warsong     3      1.83    19                  4%
      Arathi      4      2.14    22                  5%
      Dire Maul   5      2.42    25                  5%
      Gurubashi   7      2.92    30                  6%
      Stratholme  8      3.14    32                  7%
      Blackrock   9      3.35    34                  7%
      Ahn'Qiraj  10      4.35    44                  9%

  So the head gives 1% crit in Elwynn and 5% in Ahn'Qiraj; the weapon 8% damage in Elwynn,
  24% in Gurubashi and 35% in Ahn'Qiraj; the chest 15 health in Elwynn and 66 in Ahn'Qiraj.
  gear.md has the full table.

  From the second zone on (every zone but Elwynn), every piece ALSO gets a small CLASS BONUS:
  two extra stats, grown and rounded the same way. Which two depends on the class and the slot
  (the game counts: class number x 3 + slot number, and wraps around after 8). The 8 bonus
  pairs, in Elwynn values:

      0  +2% damage, +1% crit             4  +1% faster cooldowns, +2% cast twice
      1  +5 health, +1% less damage       5  +10 health, +1 health per kill
      2  +5 health, +1% faster cooldowns  6  +2% damage, +1% move speed
      3  +1% move speed, +1% crit         7  +3% area, +1% crit

  Example, a warrior's chest from Ahn'Qiraj:
      15 health x 4.53 = 68 health, and 1% x 4.53 = 5% less damage taken
      plus bonus pair 5 (warrior is class 1: 1 x 3 + chest 2 = 5): 45 health, 5 health per kill

  Only what you WEAR counts (our best reading).


OLD SAVES

  Before gear came by zone, a piece was just a tier. A save from then is changed once, when it
  is opened: every old piece becomes the piece of the LATER zone of its tier (tier 0 Elwynn,
  1 Warsong, 2 Dire Maul, 3 Stratholme, 4 Ahn'Qiraj), worn or in the collection.

  Saves from before every zone had its own look kept a chosen look as a tier (0 to 4). Such a
  look is moved on once: it becomes the look of the LATEST zone of
  that tier whose piece of the slot you have won; with none won, the choice goes and the slot
  shows its own look. Since then a save keeps a chosen look by its zone and row.

  Later saves kept pieces by number too; they are changed once in the same way, each piece
  named by its slot and its zone. Since then a save names every piece ("head" from "elwynn"),
  so when zones are added or taken out, the save follows: every piece stays the piece of the zone
  it came from, wherever that zone now sits among the others.


NAMES AND LOOKS

  - Every zone's armor pieces have their own names (Duskwood's warrior helm is the Sparkmetal
    Coif, the Scarlet Monastery's are "Adventurer's" pieces, Elwynn's the Recruit's). A weapon
    piece is "<zone> Armaments" (a rogue's or shaman's second one "<zone> Off-hand Armaments").
  - Each piece shows a WoW 3D model (a "display ID") of its zone. If a zone has no model for a
    slot (written as 0), it borrows the nearest zone's: Elwynn's starting look has no helm for
    several classes, so they show Duskwood's.
  - A look you show over a worn piece is a zone's: the slot's piece of that zone unlocks it (a
    Blackrock piece both its rows). A look you chose stays with the slot until you change it.
  - Each piece has a short flavour line in its tooltip. We write our own.

NOT KNOWN YET

  - (answered) A dropped piece goes into your collection. If you already had it, nothing
    changes. Gear is never put on automatically: equip it in the armory before the next run.


WHICH BOSS DROPS WHAT

  A boss drops gear of its own zone. Every piece it can drop gets its own dice roll (60% in
  Elwynn down to 20% in the last zones, more with harder Run Options).

  The four normal bosses share out the 8 armor slots:
    - Elwynn is the only tier-0 zone, so its 4 bosses get 2 slots each:
        boss 1: head and feet        boss 2: shoulders and wrists
        boss 3: chest and hands      boss 4: waist and legs
    - Every other tier has two zones (for example Duskwood and Warsong), so 8 bosses share
      the 8 slots: the first zone's bosses drop head, shoulders, chest and waist; the second
      zone's bosses drop legs, feet, wrists and hands. (For now: a later pass will let every
      zone drop all its pieces.)
  The final boss of a zone drops your class's weapon (rogues and shamans: both weapons).


4. THE GEAR FILE
----------------

All of this lives in ONE file: content\gear.ron. Its parts, top to bottom:

    slots               the 8 armor slots: their WoW numbers, the 4 slot words
                        (cloth, leather, mail, plate) and their Elwynn stats
    bonus_stats         the 8 bonus pairs (Elwynn values)
    sets                one block per class:
                          class, armor type,
                          the bonus pair each slot and weapon adds (bonus_pairs),
                          a look per zone (looks): the zone, its row (Blackrock's and
                            Stratholme's second is row 1), 8 names and 8 display IDs,
                          the weapons (stats, WoW weapon type, hand)
    The weapons' looks are each zone's ARMAMENTS, in contentloadouts.ron: every loadout
    weapon has a look per zone (and row), with the item's name, its display ID and, where it
    is another kind than the loadout says (an axe among swords), its own kind.

A stat is written like this:   (stat: FlatMaxHealth, value: 10.0)
Stat names you can use: Damage, CooldownRecovery, FlatMaxHealth, MaxHealth, CritChance,
CritDamage, DamageReduction, HealthPerSecond, MoveSpeed, Area, PickupRange, Gold, Experience,
Luck, DoubleCast, HealPerKill, HealAndShieldPower, ExtraTargets, and the others listed in
classes.md.
Percentages are written as fractions: 0.03 = 3%.


5. WHAT YOU CAN CHANGE
----------------------

  CHANGE WHAT A SLOT GIVES
      Edit the "stats" of that slot (all classes change together), or of a weapon.

  MAKE GEAR GROW FASTER OR SLOWER FROM ZONE TO ZONE
      Edit gear_exponent (0.55 now) in content\balance.ron, zone_scaling. A bigger number
      makes harder zones' gear stronger. Then run the balance check with --gear.

  CHANGE HOW A PIECE LOOKS, OR ITS NAME
      In gear.ron, find the class's look for that zone and replace the display ID (or the
      name) of that slot with another WoW item model that fits the same slot. A weapon look:
      the loadout weapon's line for that zone in loadouts.ron.

  GIVE A NEW ZONE ITS LOOK
      Add a look for the zone's id to every class in gear.ron, and a line for it to every
      loadout weapon in loadouts.ron. Until then the zone's pieces show the look of the zone
      before it. A second row for a zone is the same look with "row: 1".

  ADD A WEAPON LINE
      Add another weapon block to the class's set. (The planned weapon loadouts will use this.)
      A set holds at most two weapon lines (slots 8 and 9): more needs code.

  GEAR FOR A NEW CLASS
      Add a new block under "sets" for that class (copy templates/gear_set.ron). Every class
      needs exactly one.

After editing: save, then run "cargo test -p esurv-content -p esurv-sim" to check for mistakes.
