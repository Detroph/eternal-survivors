ESURV - THE GAME'S OWN WINDOWS, EXPLAINED
==============================================

This file explains in plain words the windows the game adds on top of WoW. The technical
version is screens.md in the folder above this one.


1. THE SHORT VERSION
--------------------

  Besides the normal WoW interface (action bar, character pane, chat ...), the game draws a
  few windows of its own. They only show while your hero is in the world, except the menus
  before a run (section 8), where you choose your hero. Clicking or
  scrolling on one of them never moves the camera or clicks the world behind it. They never
  take your keys, so you can keep moving while you use them, with two exceptions: the card
  window (below) takes a few keys while it is open; and Escape during a run pauses the run
  and opens the run's own menu when there is nothing else for it to close, instead of
  opening the game menu (see THE ESCAPE MENU below).

  Every window of a run except the damage meter, the card window and the Escape menu looks
  like WoW's own windows: the same stone borders, gold titles, red buttons, icon frames and
  letters, taken from your own WoW files, and the same size as WoW's windows next to them.
  The card window, the Escape menu and the menus before a run have a look of their own
  (section 8): dark panels with thin gold borders and rounded corners.


2. THE CARD WINDOW (LEVEL-UP, TREASURE CACHE, RARE'S CACHE)
---------------------------------------------------------------

  Each time your hero gains a level, each time you open a treasure cache (bought with gold)
  and each time you open a rare cache (free), the run stops, the world goes a little
  darker and a window shows a few cards side by side. Pick one; the run goes on when you do.
  Nothing can hurt you while it is open.

  The window is dark with a thin gold border, like the menus before a run. Its gold title
  says "Level 7" (your new level), "Treasure Cache" or "Rare Cache".

  On the left, "Spells": the spells you know, with their rank, and how many of your 6 spell
  slots are used ("3 / 6"); the empty slots show as empty frames. On the right, "Jewelry":
  your rings (2 slots), trinkets (2 slots) and amulet (1 slot), each with its rank. Hold the
  mouse over any of them to read what it does.

  Each card shows:
    - the number of the key that takes it, in its top left corner
    - its icon, and its name in its rarity colour: white common, green uncommon, blue
      rare, purple epic, orange legendary (the same colours as WoW's items); the card's
      border and its glow have that colour too, the colour runs down from its top edge, it
      glows brighter under the mouse and brightest once you took it
    - a small label in its top right corner: NEW for a spell you do not have yet, RING,
      TRINKET or AMULET for jewelry you do not wear yet, or RANK and the rank it reaches
      for something you take again (a spell upgrade, an upgrade of a piece you wear, an
      empowered amulet)
    - its rarity above its name, and under the name "New spell", "Ring", "Trinket",
      "Amulet", or "Rank 1 » 2" for something you take again
    - what it does. Numbers it gives you are green (like the "Equip:" lines on WoW items),
      the words white:
        - a spell you do not have yet is written out in full with its real numbers (what
          it hits, how far, how much damage, what it does to enemies, what bosses shake
          off); a rarer card adds its head start under it, for example "-7% cooldown"
        - an upgrade of a spell you know says only what gets better, for example
          "+34% damage"
        - a new ring or trinket says what it gives: "+19% critical strike"
        - an upgrade of a ring or trinket you wear says the value now and after:
          "+70% » +114% critical strike damage"
        - an amulet says its special rule first, as a sentence, then its stats
          ("+15% max health"). A legendary amulet says which of your spells it remakes and
          everything it does to it.
    - at the very bottom, in small grey letters, what it does to your slots: "Takes a spell
      slot · 3/6", "Improves a spell you know", "Takes a ring slot · 1/2", "Upgrades a
      ring you wear", "Takes the amulet slot". An amulet that would take the place of the
      one you wear says "Replaces" and that amulet's name there instead: hold the mouse
      over it to read what you would lose.
  There is no button on a card: click anywhere on it to take it.

  Under the cards:
    - the Reroll button deals new cards. It says how many free rerolls you have left,
      or what the next reroll costs in gold once they are used up; it is greyed out when
      you can do neither.
    - how much gold you have
    - a reminder of the keys

  Picking:
    1, 2, 3, 4            take that card (the number keys or the keypad's)
    R                     reroll
    a click               anywhere on a card takes it; on Reroll, rerolls
  Right after the window opens or the cards change, it waits a moment (less than half a
  second) before it listens, so a key you were already pressing for a spell does not pick
  a card by accident. While the window is open those keys do not cast your spells, and
  Escape does nothing (it does not pause or open the game menu). If you are typing in
  the chat box, your keys go to the chat as usual.


3. THE DAMAGE METER
-------------------

  The small dark window on the right side, high enough to leave the tooltips below it free. It works like the
  "Recount" add-on many WoW players know.

  What it shows:
    - "Damage Done": how much damage each hero, pet or spell has dealt this run.
    - "Damage Taken": how much damage you took, and from what. "area" means damage from
      something on the ground (a boss's blast, a volley) rather than from one enemy.

  Each line is a coloured bar. The biggest is on top and fills the whole width; the others
  are as long as their share of the biggest. Heroes have their class colour (mages light
  blue, warriors tan ...); everything else gets a colour of its own that stays the same.

  On each line, for example:   2. Frostbolt          7846 (482.1, 12%)
    2.        its place in the list
    7846      the total so far (big numbers are shortened: 153.2k, 2.35M)
    482.1     per second
    12%       its share of the whole list

  The buttons on the title bar:
    <  and  >     switch between Damage Done and Damage Taken
    R             clear the meter and start counting again
    -             fold the window down to its title bar (+ opens it again)

  Moving and sizing:
    - Drag the red title bar to move the window anywhere.
    - Drag the dotted corner (bottom right) to make it bigger or smaller.
    - With more lines than fit, a thin scroll bar appears on the right: use the mouse
      wheel over the window, or drag the bar.

  The game remembers where you put it, its size, which list it shows and whether it is
  folded, in benilla-config\meter.ron (a small text file). Delete that file to put the
  window back where it started.


4. THE END OF A RUN
-------------------

  When your hero falls, or beats the zone's final boss, a panel opens in the middle of the
  screen. It looks and reads like the normal WoW windows and grows with the game window.

  At the top, in big letters: "Overrun" (red) if you fell, "Triumph" (gold) if you won, and
  under it your hero, race, class and the zone.

  The window below (drag its title, or the big letters, to move it out of the way; the game
  remembers where you put it, next time too):

    Left side      what you did this run: how long you lasted, the level you reached, how
                   many enemies, elites, rares and bosses you defeated, the damage you
                   did and took, the gold you picked up and the caches you opened.

    Right side     the talent experience your class earned. Each part shows up one after
                   the other (time, enemies, bosses, the victory, a harder zone, harder run
                   settings), the "Earned" number counts up, and the purple bar fills toward
                   your class's next talent point. "New talent point!" flashes each time the
                   bar fills up.

    Below          a green line if the win opened the next zone for your class, then the
                   boss gear you won: each piece in its quality colour, where it is worn, and
                   whether it is new to your collection or one you already had.

  The buttons:
    Run Again      start over at once: same hero, same zone, same run settings. Everything
                   on the field disappears, your hero is back at the start with full health,
                   and the run waits for your first step.
    Go Endless     (after a win only) keep fighting: the enemies keep coming, ever stronger,
                   and what you earn from now on is paid when you fall or leave.
    Change Hero    leave the run and go back to the character screen.

  Your talents, gear and codex are not shown during a run:
  they will be on the hero sheet before a run (see THE ARMORY AND THE CODEX below).

  What you earned is already saved when the panel opens, whichever button you press.

  WoW's own "Release Spirit" box stays hidden while the panel is up: a run has no graveyard.
  After "Run Again" your hero is back at level 1; the chat no longer calls that a level-up
  (WoW's own level-up glow still flashes once).

5. THE HUD
----------

  The run's numbers, at the top in the middle of the screen, in the normal WoW lettering:

      Foes slain        12:34          Gold
         132     Boss arrives in 2:26   245
                                        Next cache: 50

    12:34                how long the run has gone (minutes:seconds)
    Boss arrives in 2:26 how long until the next boss comes. Once you went on into Endless it
                         says "Endless mode"; when no boss is left to come it says nothing.
    Foes slain           how many enemies you have killed this run
    Gold                 your gold, and under it what the next cache costs to open

  While a boss is up, a bar under the numbers shows its name (on a red strip, like the
  target frame shows an enemy) and its health in green, in a frame like the casting bar's,
  with "health / maximum" on it.
  With more than one boss up, the bar follows the one that came first.

  Big lines in the middle of the screen, under WoW's own centre messages:
    - a boss draws near (orange), the final boss arrives (red),
    - a boss is vanquished (gold). Rares come and fall without one.
  They stay 10 seconds and fade out, like WoW's raid warnings.

  Smaller yellow lines under them are the game's short messages: a magnet dropped, you took
  a rune, a cache needs more gold, you cannot leave the fight. They stay 5 seconds and fade,
  like WoW's yellow info messages. The same message coming again does not stack: it just
  starts over.

  A boss's shout shows in a speech bubble over its head; when that bubble would cover the
  numbers at the top or the boss bar, it moves down under them, so the clock stays readable.

  Before your first step a gold line pulses: take a step and the fight begins.


  THE ESCAPE MENU (PAUSING)

  During a run, Escape first does what it always does in WoW: it closes
  an open window or popup, leaves the chat line, stops a cast. When there is nothing left to
  close, instead of clearing your target or opening WoW's game menu, it pauses the run and
  opens the run's menu (the menu button on the bar does the same). Your target stays: in a
  swarm something is always hitting you, and WoW picks the attacker as your target, so
  Escape would otherwise never pause. The menu wears the new menus' look (a dark panel with a
  thin gold border), says "Run Paused" and has five buttons:

    Continue    go on (Escape does the same)
    Reset Run   end this run here and start a fresh one at once, same hero, same zone, without
                leaving the world. What the run earned so far is paid out and saved (the chat
                says how much), just as if you had left it.
    Options     the game's settings, the same panel as on the warband screen (section 8): see
                below
    End Run     end this run here: what it earned is paid out and saved, and you go back to
                character select
    Quit Game   the same, then the game closes (after the save is written)

  Options opens over the paused run, which stays paused, and the menu waits behind it. Video,
  Sound and Interface work as on the warband screen; a setting that only takes hold the next
  time a hero enters the world says "next time in the world", and after Okay the menu says, in
  gold under its buttons, what waits for later. On the Saves tab you can export the save, but
  not import one: that would replace the save in the middle of a run, so it waits for the
  warband screen, and the tab says so. "Key Bindings" opens WoW's own key binding window; close
  it (its Okay or Cancel, or Escape) and you are back on the Options panel. Okay, Cancel or
  Escape on the panel bring you back to the menu.

  You cannot pause before your first step (Escape then opens WoW's game menu as usual), while
  a level-up or cache choice is open, or once the run is won or lost (the end panel has its
  own buttons). Clicking twice does nothing more: the game waits for the first click's answer.

  With other heroes in the same run (later, with multiplayer), End Run and Quit Game take only
  you out and the others play on; Reset Run then waits until nobody else is still in the run
  (it would start their run over too), and says so.


  THE POWER BAR. The bar under your hero's health on the player frame shows what your
  starting option uses or counts, with its name and count written on it: rage (red) for the
  Warrior and the Bear, energy (yellow) for the Rogue and the Cat, and for every other option
  its own counter in its own colour (gold Holy Power, purple Insanity, teal Maelstrom Weapon
  and so on); when a counter is full its passive goes off. The Rogue's and the Cat's combo
  points are the little pips under it. Stacking buffs (Zeal, Hot Streak, Maelstrom Weapon ...)
  show in the buff frame with their counts; hover one to read what it does. Burning Embers
  also float over your hero's head.

  GLOWS. Elites wear a thin purple outline with a soft, slowly pulsing glow, the same look as
  the picked hero's gold one on the warband screen; rares and the Rare Caches they drop wear it
  in silver. Bosses and the other enemies have none, and a slain one stops glowing. The Options
  panel's Interface tab turns them all off at once ("Glow around elites and rares").


6. THE ARMORY AND THE CODEX
---------------------------

  These are not open during a run. There is no key or button
  for them in a run (N opens the talent window to look at, L nothing: section 6b). The armory
  is on the hero sheet before a run, on its Collection and Talents tabs (section 8); the codex
  is on the warband screen, at the top right (section 8). What they hold:

  THE ARMORY

  Everything your class has earned between runs. It belongs to the class, not the hero: every
  warrior you make shares the warriors' armory. Whatever you change counts from your next
  run, and the sheet says so. If the game says no to something (say, a talent
  whose row is not open yet), it says why, in red.

  What it showed, tab by tab, when it was a window (the hero sheet lays it out anew):

    Character      your class's gear as it is worn, laid out like WoW's character window:
                   head, shoulders, chest and wrists on the left, hands, waist, legs and feet
                   on the right, the weapon(s) below. Each slot shows the piece's icon and
                   name in its quality colour, and the look it shows (you can show the look of
                   another piece you own). In the middle: everything the worn gear adds
                   together, how many pieces you have collected, your talent points and the
                   zones open to your class. Hover a slot to see the piece's bonuses.

    Collection     every piece your class can win: a row per slot, a column per tier (1 to 5,
                   common to legendary). Pieces you own are in colour, the others dark. "E"
                   marks what is worn, "L" the piece whose look the slot shows. Click a piece
                   to see what it gives and which boss drops it, and to Wear it (or Take Off
                   the one worn) or show its look on that slot (Show This Look; Show Own Look
                   goes back to the worn piece's own look).

    Drop Tables    one zone a page (the arrows at the top turn the pages): each boss, when it
                   arrives, and the pieces it can drop for your class, owned (green) or still
                   to find. The top says how likely each piece is to drop (harder run options
                   raise it); each piece rolls on its own, and one you already have adds
                   nothing. A zone your class has not opened says what to win to open it. The
                   bottom line counts the pieces still to find in your open zones.

    Talents        your talent points (to spend, earned out of 51, spent) and a purple bar
                   toward the next point, then your class's three talent trees side by side,
                   as WoW's talent window draws them. Click a talent to learn a rank,
                   right-click it to take one back (taking back is free). Green: you can buy a
                   rank; gold: all ranks bought; dimmed: its row or the talent it needs is not
                   there yet (a row opens for every 5 points spent higher up in its tree).
                   Hover a talent to see what it does now and at its next rank, and what it
                   still needs.

  THE CODEX

  A book of every ring, trinket and amulet any of your heroes has ever taken in a run. It is
  shared by the whole save and never forgets. Four tabs: Rings, Trinkets, Amulets and
  Legendary; each tab says how many of its kind you have found ("Rings 3/9"). Found pieces
  show their icon, name and what they do; the others are dark, with where they come from
  (caches, bought or a rare's). Legendary amulets remake one spell each, so they are listed
  class by class (the arrows turn from class to class, starting with your hero's class).
  The new menus give it a look of its own.


6b. WOW'S OWN WINDOWS DURING A RUN
----------------------------------

  During a run (and on the end-of-run panel) WoW's interface keeps only
  three of its windows:

    C   the character window, on its first tab only (no Pet, Reputation, Skills or Honor
        tabs). Its stat lines show your hero's run numbers instead of WoW's: health, damage,
        cooldowns, crit and crit power, damage reduction, regeneration, area, extra targets,
        double cast, speed and pickup range. A number your worn gear adds to is green, and
        pointing at a line says what it does and what your gear adds to it.
    P   the spellbook, with one tab, General, holding exactly your race's spell, your race's
        passives and the spells you learned in this run
    N   the talent window, to look at only: your class's talents bought so far, each in its
        place on its tree, with what it does when you point at it. Nothing can be learned or
        changed during a run: that happens on the hero sheet before a run. It opens at any
        level, and so does its button on the bar.

  Everything else is closed off: the quest log (L), the social window (O), the world map (M),
  reputation (U), skills (K), honor (H), the bags (B, F8 to F12, Shift-B) and the keyring,
  the pet spellbook, the battleground map and scores, the macro window (/macro) and the help
  window. Their keys do nothing, and their buttons are gone from the bar: only the character,
  spellbook and talent buttons stay (Escape still opens the run's menu). On the minimap the
  world map button is gone; the zoom buttons and the rest stay. Right-clicking your portrait,
  your target or a party member opens no menu.

  Pointing at a spell on the action bar or in the spellbook shows what it does in this run, in
  WoW's usual tooltip: its name and rank (Rank 1, Rank 2 ... as level-up cards upgrade it),
  its range (or the radius it covers) and school, its cooldown, and what it does with your
  hero's numbers right now (the same words as its card, with your talents, amulet, level and
  buffs counted in). Under that, when there is something to say:
    Upgrades this run   what the upgrade cards you took for it add up to: "+8% damage, +18% area"
    Your bonuses        (green) what your talents, gear, rings, trinkets, amulet and race add to
                        it: "+31% damage, -4% cooldown"
    and its crit        "7% chance to crit for 204% damage"; spells whose hits never crit
                        (damage over time, heals, shields, buffs) have no crit line
  Your race's spell says it is your race's, and your race's passives say what they give. The
  spellbook writes the same names and ranks under the icons. Before, these were WoW's own texts
  of the spells our spells look like.

  Pointing at one of your buffs (the icons at the top right, or on the target frame when you
  target yourself) says what it is: its name (the spell that cast it, or "Speed Rune",
  "Berserker Rune" ...), what it does right now ("+60% movement speed", "+42% damage", "Absorbs
  54 more damage"), the spell's own words with your numbers, whose race it belongs to for your
  race's spell, and how long it has left. Pointing at an enemy's debuff that one of your spells
  put on it (a bleed, a burn ...) shows that spell's name and what it does; anything else there
  keeps WoW's own text.

  Your buffs at the top right (runes, shields, your race's spell ...) show how long they still
  last under each icon, and blink when they are about to end; WoW keeps this off unless you
  turn it on, a run turns it on for you.

  The experience bar keeps going up to the run's own level cap (99), not WoW's 60. WoW's own
  "Congratulations, you have reached level ..." lines stay out of the chat during a run: the
  game says what each level brought in a line of its own.

  Outside a run (the character screens) nothing of this applies.


7. THE CREATE SCREEN
--------------------

  New heroes are made on the hero sheet (section 8). WoW's own create screen only shows with
  ESURV_MENUS=0 (a testing switch). Both offer, besides WoW's usual choices, six
  more: Dwarf Shaman, Human Hunter, Gnome Priest, Orc Mage,
  Troll Warlock and Undead Paladin. Pick the race and the class button shows up with the
  others, with WoW's usual description. The hero on the stage wears the class's starting
  clothes borrowed from another race (classes.txt, 3b, says which). For a test with nobody at
  the mouse:

    WOW_CHARCREATE_SHOT=1            open the create screen by itself
    WOW_CHARCREATE_PICK=3,0,7        pick race 3 (Dwarf), male (0, or 1 for female) and
                                     class 7 (Shaman)
    WOW_CHARCREATE_SHOT_OUT=<png>    take a picture of it once it has settled
    WOW_CHARCREATE_NAME=Anvilbeard   type that name and press Create


8. THE MENUS BEFORE A RUN (CHOOSING YOUR HERO)
-----------------------------------------------

  Before a run the game shows its own menus instead of WoW's character screens.
  They have a look of their own, not WoW's: near-black panels with a thin gold
  border and round corners, gold titles in WoW's Morpheus letters, small grey labels over
  white values, values in little framed boxes, a soft gold glow round what is picked, gold
  arrows, and framed buttons (the one that is on is filled dark red).

  WHEN THE GAME STARTS

  If your save has a hero, you land on the warband screen (below). If it has none, you land
  on the class picker.

  THE CLASS PICKER

  One panel with the nine classes in three rows of three. Click one to read about it: how it
  plays, its health, the spell every run starts with, how many zones your class has opened
  ("1 of 9"), and every spell it can learn in a run (hover one for what it is). "Choose" opens
  the hero sheet for a new hero of that class; "Back" returns to your heroes.

  THE HERO SHEET

  Where a new hero is made, and where "Modify selected character" takes you. Three tabs at the
  top right; "Back" at the top left leaves without making or changing anything.

    Character      on the left, the races as portraits (Alliance, then Horde; the ones your
                   class cannot be are dimmed), Male and Female, and the gear your class wears,
                   slot by slot, with what it adds. In the middle the hero's name (just type
                   it: letters only, up to twelve), and the hero itself on a lit floor: drag it
                   or use the arrows to turn it. On the right the looks: skin colour, face, hair
                   style, hair colour and the race's own extra (beard, piercings, tusks ...),
                   each with arrows and dots, and Randomize. "Create" makes the hero; "Save
                   changes" keeps the changes of a hero you modify. A hero's race and looks can
                   always change before a run; a hero who becomes Alliance or Horde moves to that
                   camp.

    Collection     every piece of gear your class can win, a column per slot and a row per
                   quality (common to legendary), with how many you have found and the zones
                   that drop them; a dark piece with "?" is not found yet, E marks what is worn,
                   L the look a slot shows. Below: what your open zones still hold (the piece,
                   the boss, the zone, the chance). Click a piece for what it adds and who drops
                   it, and to wear it or show its look; click a slot on the left to choose the
                   look it shows.

    Talents        your class's three talent trees on their WoW pictures, each talent with its
                   rank; lines join a talent to the one it needs. On the right your points to
                   spend, earned and spent, the bar to the next point, Reset (everything back),
                   and the talent you clicked with Learn and Unlearn.

  Gear and talents belong to the class: every warrior shares the warriors'. They change at once
  and count from the next run; while you make a new hero, the sheet reminds you that they stay
  even if you go back without making it.

  THE WARBAND SCREEN

  Your heroes wait around a campfire in the real world, one camp for each side:

    Horde      beside the road in front of the main gate of Orgrimmar, at night; the heroes
               kneel around the campfire
    Alliance   on the grass beside the main gate of Stormwind, at night; the heroes sit
               around the campfire

  The camp fills the lower left of the window. Each hero wears the gear its class is wearing;
  where the class wears nothing in a slot, the hero shows the clothes WoW gives a new hero of
  that race and class. Up to nine heroes a side: the first five wait in a half circle round
  the fire and stand up when you point at them or pick them; the other four stand behind them
  and salute (warriors, paladins, hunters, rogues) or wave when picked. Click a hero to pick
  it; its name shows over the fire, and a thin gold outline with a soft, slowly pulsing glow
  goes round it (and only it), its gear and weapons too, following it as it stands up and
  greets you.

    - top left: the picked hero's name, race and class, its total wins and runs, then its
      starting spell: the class's three starting options as icons (four for the Druid, its
      forms). Hover one to read what it does, with its numbers, and its passive; click it to
      pick it. The hero keeps the pick for its next runs (the first one until you pick).
      Below it a place kept for later (choosing its weapons)
    - top middle: the armour it wears (hover a piece for its name)
    - right: the zones. The zone of the hero's next run is open as a card: what it is, its
      enemies and its bosses with the time each one comes. Click another zone to send the
      hero there; a zone your class has not opened says which boss to beat, and where, to
      open it. Below: "Difficulty modifiers" (the run options: more, tougher, harder-hitting
      or faster enemies for more rewards; they count for every hero) and "Start run"
    - top right, beside the zones: the codex, with how many rings, trinkets, amulets and
      legendary amulets you have found across all your heroes, and "Open the codex". The
      codex opens over the screen: a tab per list with its count, arrows through the pages
      (the legendary amulets a class a page, your picked hero's class first), and every
      piece in two columns. A found piece shows its icon, its name and what it does; one not
      found is dark, its name grey, with where it comes from. "Close" or Escape closes it.
    - the round button at the camp's top left shows the other side's camp; its little badge
      says how many heroes wait there
    - under the campfire: "New character", "Modify character" and "Delete character".
      Deleting asks first ("No" is the default); after a delete the camp picks the next hero.
    - bottom-left corner: "Options" above "Quit game". Quit game closes the game. When a newer
      version of the game is out, a small panel above them says "v0.5.0 is available" with an
      "Update" button: it downloads the new version, checks it and restarts the game into it,
      your heroes and settings kept (server.txt, UPDATES). If it fails, the panel says why and
      nothing changes; Update tries again.

  Options holds the game's own settings in three tabs, as WoW's own options windows do (those
  only exist once a hero is in the world), and a fourth for your save:

    Video       the display (resolution, multisampling, windowed, vertical sync and the
                rest), the interface scale, brightness, and the world's detail: view
                distance, ground and texture detail, spell effects, weather and the shaders
    Sound       sound, music, ambience and their volumes; these change as you move them
    Interface   four headed sections, in this order (roll the mouse wheel over them if they
                don't all fit):
                  Mouse & Camera     inverting the mouse, click to move, the mouse and
                                     camera speeds, how the camera follows, its distance
                                     and the camera boxes
                  Names              the names over heads
                  Eternal Survivors  the game's own settings, below, and the boss
                                     warnings: the warnings, their sound, the boss timer
                                     bars, showing their handles (hidden, hold Shift to
                                     drag the bars) and putting the bars back
                  Misc               chat bubbles, clicking the ground to clear the
                                     target, clearing away, tips on loading screens and

                "Glow around elites and rares" (on at first): elites glow purple around
                their outline, and rares and the Rare Caches they drop glow silver, so the
                strong enemies stand out in a crowd. Bosses and other enemies never glow.
                Unticked, every glow hides at once.
                "Damage numbers" is Smart or All: Smart, the default, hides your small hits. Every 30
                seconds the game takes the average of your hits, each counted as much as it
                deals, and for the next 30 seconds hides the numbers below 60 % of it, so only
                your bigger hits show (in the first 30 seconds every number shows). Crits must pass
                it too, but count in the average only when your crit chance is over 50 %.
                Each number is one hit: each target of a spell that hits many, and each
                tick of damage over time, so small ticks mostly hide.
                All shows every number. The damage itself, the meter and the combat log never
                change, and numbers over your own hero always show.
                "Check for updates" (on at first): when the game starts it asks GitHub
                whether a newer version is out, and the character screen offers it. Unticked,
                the game never asks.
    Saves      "Export save" writes a copy of your whole save (every hero, what each class
                earned, the collection, the codex) to a folder named on the page, the file
                named with the date and time. The saves in that folder are listed, newest
                first. Pick one and press "Import save" to replace your save with it: the game
                asks first, checks the file, and keeps your current save in the same folder
                (its name says "before-import"), so you can import it back. To bring a save
                from another computer, put its file in that folder. Only a game running on
                your own computer can do this.

  Arrows change each setting; a setting greys out while the one it hangs on is off. "Okay"
  keeps the changes, "Cancel" (or Escape) drops them. A line says which settings only take
  hold after a restart or when a hero next enters the world. Key bindings are set during a
  run: Escape, then Options, then Key Bindings (section 5, the Escape menu).

  Escape does nothing else here.

  The talent experience, the zones, the records and the run options come from your save
  when the game runs on your own computer; a game hosted elsewhere cannot show them yet.
